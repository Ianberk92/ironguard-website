# Outreach Templates (CEO voice) — no message is sent without explicit CEO approval

These are templates. Personalized versions for specific candidates live in `outreach/` (not committed).
Tone: a business owner who sees real potential in another successful professional. Short, specific, honest.
Never: generic recruiter language, claims about the candidate's current situation, promises of equity, titles or
income, or requests for a current employer's information.

## Channel guidance

| Situation | Channel |
|---|---|
| Public LinkedIn profile, no mutual contact | LinkedIn connection note (≤ 300 characters) followed by a message after acceptance |
| Mutual contact exists | Warm introduction request to the mutual contact first |
| Candidate is a public speaker/podcast guest | Reference the specific talk/episode in the first line |
| Executive at a competitor | LinkedIn only; no company email; keep it about them, not their employer |

## Connection note (≤ 300 characters)

> Hi [First name], I run Ironguard IT in Carlsbad. Your [specific public achievement, e.g., "multi-year President's Club record at X"] stood out. I'm building the commercial side of a company that publishes its pricing and owns outcomes. Open to a short conversation? — Ian

## First message after connection

> [First name], thanks for connecting. Short version: Ironguard is a senior-led managed IT and security firm in Carlsbad serving 50–500-person multi-site companies. We publish our pricing, we don't lock clients in, and we win on ownership rather than price. What we don't have yet is a true new-business leader. I'm not posting a job; I'm looking for one person who has personally built a book in recurring services and wants to shape how a company sells, with earnings tied directly to what they bring in. [One sentence on why them, from public evidence.] If that's worth 20 minutes, I'd value your perspective either way. — Ian Berkowitz, Founder & CEO, Ironguard IT

## Follow-up (7–10 days later)

> [First name], circling back once. If the timing is wrong, no problem at all; I'd still welcome a conversation about how [their market/segment] is buying managed services right now. If it's useful, here is how we price and what we commit to in writing: [pricing page link]. — Ian

## Suggested opening conversation (20–30 min)

1. Their story: how they built their current book; what they are proud of.
2. What they would want to know about Ironguard before taking it seriously (answer with facts; share the commercial profile).
3. The opportunity as it truly is: founding commercial role, direct partnership with the CEO, uncapped plan, path to leadership earned on results, no promises beyond that.
4. Their economics today, in ranges, and what a move would need to look like.
5. Agree a next step: a working session on the first-90-days plan, or a respectful close.

## Questions to test genuine interest and fit

- "What would have to be true for you to leave a role where you are already successful?"
- "If you joined and the first six months were slower than planned, what would you expect from me, and what would you do?"
- "Which part of our model would you change on day one, and why?"
- "Tell me about the last time you built something from nothing."
- "How do you think about the difference between a $110/user MSP and a $180/user MSP when you sell?"
- "What are your personal income needs during ramp, honestly?"
- "Who are three people who could speak to business you personally originated?"

## Legal and ethical guardrails

- California generally does not enforce non-competes, but non-solicitation of employees, confidentiality and trade-secret duties still apply; never ask for or accept customer lists, pricing or pipeline data from a prior employer.
- Keep outreach factual about Ironguard; do not characterize the candidate's employer.
- Record every contact and approval in the database (`contact_status`, `outreach_approval_status`).
