Trigger events the site already speaks to (useful for a hunter's prospecting lists): cyber-insurance renewal with questions the company cannot answer; "our IT person left"; opening a second site; MSP that "closes tickets but nothing gets fixed"; Google Workspace shops that Microsoft-only MSPs serve badly; fast growth that broke informal onboarding/licensing.

**Key commercial implication:** the deal size is unusually large for an MSP. Many SMB-focused MSPs average roughly $3K–$8K MRR per client; Ironguard's floor is 25 users × $150 = $3,750 MRR and its sweet spot (50–150 users) is $7.5K–$30K MRR. Fewer, larger, longer-cycle deals favor a consultative enterprise-style hunter over a high-velocity SMB closer.

## 3. Strongest potential differentiators (as a seller would use them)

1. **Published pricing** ($150–$200/user) and a defined exclusions list. Removes "what will this really cost" friction and signals confidence. Rare among MSPs; the site makes it a wedge.
2. **No lock-in**: 90-day initial term then month-to-month with 60 days' notice, and the client owns credentials/documentation from day one. Directly neutralizes the "switching is risky" objection.
3. **Written operating commitments** (same-day offboarding, day-one onboarding, quarterly restore validation, monthly license reconciliation, documentation a successor could use). These become checkable proof points in competitive displacement.
4. **Senior-led delivery**: "the person who scopes your environment is the person who runs it." Strong for closing; a capacity constraint for scaling (see obstacles).
5. **Google Workspace fluency** alongside Microsoft 365, which the site explicitly positions against Microsoft-only MSPs.
6. **Insurance Readiness Review**: a fixed-fee, low-risk land offer credited toward onboarding. Ideal for a hunter: a $1,500+ "paid discovery" that creates the environment review, the gap list and the conversion path.
7. **Founder operator credibility** in engineering/construction multi-site companies.
8. **Existing demand-generation assets**: 2-minute IT Ownership Scorecard, MSP evaluation guide, executive IT ownership checklist (lead magnet), insights library, published FAQ. A new seller does not start with a blank website.
9. **AI governance and co-managed lanes** as modern, differentiated conversation openers with COOs and internal IT leads.

## 6. The most credible recruiting value proposition

What can be said truthfully today:

- **Founding commercial leader.** There is no sales organization; the first hire shapes the go-to-market, the compensation plan, the tooling and eventually the team. Direct, daily relationship with the CEO.
- **A product that is easy to sell honestly.** Published pricing, written standards, no lock-in and client-owned credentials give a seller an unusually clean story against incumbents.
- **Larger-than-typical MSP deals** ($90K–$700K ARR), which suit an experienced mid-market hunter and make a meaningful uncapped commission plan affordable.
- **Senior engineers on sales calls** ("no sales queue"), so the seller is not alone in discovery and technical credibility is high.
- **Marketing assets already built** (Scorecard, guide, lead magnet, insights) that a seller can use immediately.
- **Upside beyond commission**: a performance-based path to a sales-leadership title and to long-term participation in the company's success, if and only if the CEO authorizes it (nothing of this kind may be promised in outreach).

What cannot be claimed: guaranteed income, equity, an executive title, certifications not published, client counts, or delivery capacity for a specific growth number until the CEO confirms it.

## 8. Commercial requirements for the hire (what the research targets)

- **Must have:** personally originated net-new B2B deals of $50K–$500K ARR with 3–9-month cycles; sold recurring services (managed IT, MSSP, cloud, UCaaS or similar); comfortable with owners, CFOs and COOs; evidence of attainment (President's Club, published rankings, promotions, awards) that can be reference-checked.
- **Strongly preferred:** MSP/MSSP experience; Southern California network; experience at a $5M–$30M firm where the seller carried a meaningful share of growth; evidence of building process (CRM discipline, prospecting cadence, partner channels).
- **For the Builder archetype:** hired/coached sellers, designed comp plans, owned forecasting, built partner programs, and sold personally while doing so.
- **Disqualifiers:** titles without attributable production; pure account management/renewals; vendor channel-management roles without a new-logo quota; patterns of short tenures with no attainment evidence (to be examined, not assumed).
