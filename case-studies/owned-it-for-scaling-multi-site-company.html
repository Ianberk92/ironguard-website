Case study · Managed IT operations

From reactive support to owned IT at a fast-scaling company

Anonymized client·Ongoing engagement·2 min read
Book a Meeting → Take the 2-minute Scorecard

A rapidly growing, multi-site company had outgrown the reactive support model: technology decisions were scattered across vendors, sites, and stretched internal staff while headcount and locations kept climbing. Ironguard took ownership of the full operating model — employee support, Microsoft 365, security baseline, device lifecycle, and vendor management — and turned a collection of point solutions into one accountable system.

The customer

A multi-site company scaling past 300 people, operating across offices, labs and remote sites in the United States and internationally. A demanding workforce, a Microsoft 365 environment, and growth fast enough that every informal process was breaking at once.

The problem

Nothing was on fire — and that was the trap. Tickets got answered. Tools existed. But onboarding went differently every time, device purchasing was ad hoc, licensing had drifted from reality, and every technology question eventually landed on leadership's desk because nobody else owned the whole picture. The company had IT activity without IT ownership.

The business risk

At this scale, the gaps compound: an inconsistent employee lifecycle becomes a security exposure; untracked renewals become budget surprises; each new site launched without a standard becomes a permanent support tax. The real risk was not an outage — it was growth itself turning technology into the company's bottleneck.

Ironguard's assessment

The diagnosis was ownership, not tooling. The environment needed one operating model: a single accountable owner for support, identity, devices, security, and vendors, with standards strong enough that the next hundred hires and the next several sites would land on rails instead of improvisation.

What we did

Ironguard took over day-to-day employee support with clear escalation, then standardized the foundations underneath it: identity and MFA enforcement in Entra ID, device standards with managed enrollment through Intune, a defined onboarding and offboarding path, licensing reconciled to actual usage, and a renewal calendar covering software, hardware, and vendor contracts. Vendor relationships moved behind one throat to choke: ours.

The complexity handled

This was not a greenfield: the work happened while the company kept hiring, opening sites, and running day-to-day operations across time zones, with a workforce that notices every rough edge. Changes were sequenced so that no team lost a working day to the transition.

The result

Leadership stopped being the escalation path. New hires land with working equipment and correct access on day one. Licensing and renewals stopped generating surprises. And when something breaks, there is one owner, a visible status, and a next step — the operating definition of IT that is actually owned.

Where it stands

The engagement is ongoing: Ironguard continues to own support, security, lifecycle, and planning as the company scales.
