IT After the Raise · Life sciences, biotech & medtech

The round has closed. Get IT ready before the hiring wave lands.

A raise usually brings more people, more instruments, more data and harder security questions from investors and partners, all in the same few months. These are the things to put in order in the weeks after the money arrives. Tick what is done; put a name next to what is not.

01

Onboarding for the hiring wave

  • One written joiner and leaver checklist, owned by HR and IT together
  • Start dates reach IT early enough to order and set up equipment
  • Access by role: what a scientist, a QA lead and an operations hire each get
  • Same-day removal of access when someone leaves, contractors included
02

Laptops ready on day one

  • A short list of standard models, ordered ahead of start dates, with a small spare pool
  • Devices enrolled in management so they arrive configured, not set up by hand
  • Encryption, updates and endpoint protection on before the first sign-in
  • A named person the new hire can call on their first morning
03

Identity and multi-factor sign-in

  • One company identity per person (Microsoft 365 or Google), used for every app that supports it
  • MFA on every account; phishing-resistant methods for administrators
  • Separate admin accounts, and a short list of who holds them
  • Shared lab and instrument logins found and replaced where the software allows
04

Lab instruments on their own network

  • An inventory of instrument PCs: what each runs, its operating system, who supports it
  • Instruments on a separate network segment, allowed only the connections they need
  • Vendor remote access through one controlled, logged path, not an always-on tool
  • Instrument PCs that cannot be updated are isolated, not ignored
05

Research data backed up, restores tested

  • A map of where research data lives: instruments, file shares, cloud, ELN and LIMS
  • Instrument output copied off the instrument PC on a schedule
  • At least one copy offline or immutable, out of reach of normal sign-ins
  • A restore tested on a schedule, with how long it took written down
06

Change control for validated and quality systems

  • A list of the systems in scope for your quality system or GxP (QMS, LIMS, ELN, instrument software)
  • IT changes to those systems follow your change control: request, impact, test, approval, record
  • Update and patch windows agreed with Quality, not decided by IT alone
  • Tickets, approvals and test results kept where an auditor can find them

07

Investor and partner security questions

  • Expect questions on MFA, devices, backups, access reviews, incident response and vendors
  • A short written security summary and current policies, ready to share
  • An incident response plan that says who calls whom and who decides
  • Security clauses in partner and customer agreements listed, with what each commits you to
  • Cyber insurance answers that match what is actually in place
08

Vendor and SaaS sprawl

  • Every SaaS app listed with an owner; card and expense data is the fastest way to find them
  • Sign-in through your company identity (single sign-on) where the app offers it
  • Duplicate tools and unused licenses removed
  • For each vendor holding research or patient data: where it is stored and what the contract says
The 30/60/90-day order of work

Protect everything else first (sign-in, backups, onboarding), then separate the lab, bring Quality in, and write the diligence answers down.

Days 1 to 30

Lock the front door

  • MFA on every account; admin accounts separated
  • Joiner and leaver checklist in use for every hire
  • Laptop standard chosen; orders placed ahead of start dates
  • Research data locations mapped; backups confirmed
  • First restore test done and timed
  • Instrument PCs and SaaS apps inventoried, each with an owner
Days 31 to 60

Separate and standardize

  • Lab network separation planned with lab leads and instrument vendors, then started
  • Change control for quality and GxP systems agreed with Quality
  • Every laptop enrolled in device management
  • Vendor remote access moved to one logged path
  • Single sign-on for the main SaaS apps
  • Security summary and policies drafted for diligence
Days 61 to 90

Prove it works

  • Lab network separation finished and tested
  • Second restore test, compared with the first
  • Access review: who has what, and who should not
  • Unused SaaS and licenses removed
  • Incident response walk-through with leadership
  • A quarterly review set, so this stays true after the next hire
