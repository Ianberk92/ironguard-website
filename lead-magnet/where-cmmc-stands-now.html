Where CMMC stands now · As of November 26, 2026

CMMC Phase 2 is paused. Your cybersecurity obligations are not.

In July 2026 the Department of Defense (also styled the Department of War) suspended CMMC Phase 2: contract-required certification assessments by an accredited third-party assessor (C3PAO), due to start on November 10, 2026. Here is what changed, what still applies, and what to do next.

Paused

C3PAO assessments as a condition of contract award, and the later phases built on them. Contracting officers were told to remove those requirements from affected solicitations and contracts.1,2

Still in force

DFARS 252.204-7012, NIST SP 800-171, your SPRS self-assessment score, CMMC Level 1 and Level 2 self-assessments and affirmations where your contract requires them, FAR 52.204-21, and flow-down to subcontractors.1,2,3

What changed, in order
  • July 13, 2026. DoD announced the suspension of Phase 2 and a 60-day review by a CMMC Reform Task Force. While it holds, solicitations may require only CMMC Level 1 (Self) or Level 2 (Self) assessments.1,2
  • September 3, 2026. A DoD class deviation made the change binding on contracting officers: third-party assessment requirements come out of contracts.4
  • October 2026. Press reports describe the task force’s options, including restarting Phase 2 later for a narrower group of contractors.5 As of November 26, 2026 we have not seen an official new Phase 2 date.
What still applies now
01

DFARS 7012: safeguarding and reporting

  • DFARS 252.204-7012: protect covered defense information (CUI) using NIST SP 800-171
  • Report cyber incidents to DoD within 72 hours, and keep images and logs for DoD to review
  • Flow the clause down to subcontractors whose work involves that information
02

NIST SP 800-171: the 110 requirements

  • Rev. 2’s 110 security requirements are still the standard for Level 2 and for 7012
  • A System Security Plan (SSP) and a plan of action (POA&M) for anything not yet met
03

SPRS score (DFARS 7019 and 7020)

  • DFARS 252.204-7019/-7020: a self-assessment scored with the DoD method, posted in SPRS and kept current for award
  • DoD can still review or audit that score; newer contracts may show renumbered clauses under DoD’s FAR-overhaul deviations1
04

Affirmations and flow-down

  • Where a contract includes DFARS 252.204-7021: a current Level 1 or Level 2 (Self) status and an annual affirmation by a senior official
  • FAR 52.204-21’s 15 basic safeguards for federal contract information
  • Primes still have to flow requirements down and get assurances from their suppliers1,2

What primes are still asking for
  • Your SPRS score and its date, and whether it matches your evidence.
  • Your SSP and POA&M, or a summary, on request.
  • Signed attestations and security questionnaires covering 7012 and flow-down.
  • Incident reporting readiness: who reports, and how, within 72 hours.
  • Sometimes a Level 2 certificate anyway. Some primes may keep asking subcontractors for C3PAO audits despite the pause.3 Voluntary assessments are a business decision, not a current DoD contract requirement.

Why the pause raises the stakes

With fewer third-party checks, DoD leans more on what contractors say about themselves. The Justice Department’s Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative uses the False Claims Act against companies that misstate their cybersecurity compliance, so an inflated score or affirmation is a real risk.1,4

Readiness checklist
  • Know where CUI and federal contract information live: people, systems, sites and suppliers
  • List the clauses in each contract: 7012, 7019, 7020, 7021, FAR 52.204-21 (or their new numbers)
  • Run an honest NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment, requirement by requirement, with evidence for each
  • Post or refresh your SPRS score, and record the date and who approved it
  • Bring the SSP and POA&M up to date; give each open item an owner and a date
  • Check your 72-hour incident reporting path works before you need it
  • Confirm that subcontractors handling CUI have the clauses flowed down and current scores
  • Brief whoever signs affirmations on what they are signing
  • Keep building toward Level 2 as if assessments return; the requirements underneath have not changed
