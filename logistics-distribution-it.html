For logistics, warehousing and distribution companies with more than one building: Wi-Fi that reaches the far end of the racking, scanners that just work, a WMS and ERP that stay up, internet that fails over on its own, and a named owner for every part of your technology.

When this fits

When the scanners stop, the trucks stop.

Warehouses are hard on technology. Wi-Fi drops between the racks. Scanners and rugged tablets get dropped, swapped and lost. The WMS or ERP goes down and orders pile up at the dock. A single internet line feeds a building that ships all night. New shift workers start on Monday and need a login on Monday. Cameras and badge readers sit on the same network as the office PCs.

None of that waits for business hours, and none of it should depend on one person who knows how it all connects.

We take it over and run it as one system. Ironguard IT is based in Carlsbad, in Southern California, and supports companies across California.

What we take on

Every building connected. Every device accounted for.

Managed service starts at 25 users. If you have an internal IT lead, we work through them, as owned lanes, not around them.

Warehouses and networks

  • Warehouse Wi-Fi designed for racking, freezers and dock doors
  • One firewall and network standard at every building
  • Internet failover, so a cut line does not stop shipping
  • Cameras, badge readers and building systems on their own network
  • WMS and ERP uptime: servers, integrations, licensing and the vendor relationship

Devices and people

  • Scanners, rugged tablets and label printers enrolled, tracked and replaced fast
  • Shift-based onboarding, so a new hire has a login on their first shift
  • Shared devices set up so shifts can hand over without sharing passwords
  • A security baseline: identity and MFA, devices, email and backup
  • Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace administration and licensing
Your first 90 days with us

What happens after you hire us.

A typical start. Your own plan is written down, with names and dates, before any work begins.

  1. Week 1

    We walk every building, on every shift.

    • Walk each warehouse with your operations lead, including the night shift
    • List every scanner, tablet, printer, camera and badge reader
    • Map the network, the internet lines and what the WMS depends on
    • Take over admin accounts and put them in your company’s name

    Where you are: A written map of every building, and the three biggest risks named.

  2. Days 1–30

    Stop the outages that stop shipping.

    • Wi-Fi survey of the floor, and the worst dead spots fixed
    • Backup internet installed or tested at every building
    • WMS and ERP servers backed up, and a real restore tested
    • Cameras and badge readers moved onto their own network

    Where you are: A cut line or a dead access point no longer stops the dock.

  3. Days 31–60

    Make devices and logins routine.

    • Every scanner and tablet enrolled, so a lost one can be locked and replaced
    • A spare pool of devices, set up and ready to swap
    • Shift onboarding and offboarding on a checklist, every day of the week
    • MFA for office and remote accounts, without slowing the floor

    Where you are: New shift workers are productive on day one, and leavers lose access the same day.

  4. Days 61–90

    Plan the next building.

    • A launch checklist for your next warehouse or cross-dock
    • Disaster recovery plan for the WMS, written and walked through
    • Device refresh and carrier contracts on one calendar
    • Your first quarterly review: what changed, what is next, what it costs

    Where you are: The next site opens on schedule, built to the same standard as the rest.

Common questions

Straight answers for logistics and distribution companies.

Can you fix Wi-Fi in a warehouse?

Yes. We survey the floor, then design the Wi-Fi for your racking, ceiling height and dock doors, so scanners stay connected from one end of the building to the other. Hardware and cabling are scoped separately, in writing.

Do you manage scanners and rugged devices?

Yes. Every scanner, rugged tablet and label printer is enrolled and tracked. A lost or broken device can be locked and swapped for a spare that is already set up.

What happens if our internet goes down?

Each building gets a second internet line that takes over on its own when the first one fails, so the WMS, scanners and label printers keep working.

We hire by shift and turnover is high. Can you keep up?

Yes. New hires get a login on their first shift and lose access the day they leave, from the same checklist every time. Shared devices are set up so shifts can hand over without sharing passwords.

What does it cost?

Three published levels, $115 to $200 per user per month. Companies with several buildings, servers or a WMS usually fit the Managed level. Hardware, cabling, new buildings and major projects are scoped separately, in writing, before work begins.
